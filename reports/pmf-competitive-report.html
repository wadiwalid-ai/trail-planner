The lens — Alstromer / YC

PMF isn't decided in a document. It's measured.

Gustaf Alstromer's whole point: product–market fit is not something you argue on a slide — it's something real users prove. The cleanest test in the industry is a single question.

"How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?"
PMF ≈ when ≥ 40% say "very disappointed." (Sean Ellis test, used across YC.)

Honest headline: you don't have PMF yet — nobody does before users. What you can do now is pick the market where the fit hypothesis is strongest and incumbents weakest, ship to a narrow segment, and run the 40% test.

Bottom line up front

Win MENA first, then expand.

Fighting worldwide head-on against AllTrails + Gaia + onX + Wikiloc is a knife fight you'll probably lose. MENA is wide open — the global leaders barely serve it. That's where "a gazillion times better than Gaia & Wikiloc" is actually achievable, because in the desert they're not really trying.

Positioning — April Dunford method

Start from the alternatives, not the features.

Positioning is relative. Define yourself against what a Gulf off-roader actually does today — then the value becomes obvious.

1Competitive alternatives. Gaia (English, generic), Maps.me/OsmAnd (offline but dumb), WhatsApp groups + a guide's tribal knowledge, or nothing — just follow a friend who "knows the dunes."
2Unique attributes. AI planning · Arabic-first · desert/dune guidance · integrated safety/SOS + convoy tracking · one modern app instead of three clunky ones.
3Value delivered. Plan a safe desert run in minutes, not hours of forum-digging; don't get lost or stranded with no signal; bring the family/club safely.
4Best-fit beachhead. GCC weekend off-roaders & 4x4 clubs — Saudi (Zulfi, Riyadh) and UAE (Liwa, Al Qudra). 25–45, premium SUV, social, safety-conscious, pays for apps.

07
"A gazillion times better than Gaia & Wikiloc"

Concrete moves.

Ranked by impact-to-effort. The first three are the wedge; the rest widen the moat.

  1. Own desert safety. Offline-first SOS with plus-codes, live location share, group/convoy tracking, "tell someone before you go" check-in, recovery-point intelligence, live off-camber & rollover-risk alerts from the vehicle's tilt, and community "heads-up" hazard waypoints — soft drop-offs, sabkha/salt-bog, sharp rock, washouts, fence lines, camel crossings — kept trustworthy with community "still there?" confirms + expiry, and approved-marshal reports weighted highest. This is the "would die without it" feature Gaia & Wikiloc simply don't have.
  2. AI planning that's conditions-aware — and terrain-aware for beginners & families. "Plan me a 3-hour intermediate dune run near Liwa this weekend, family-safe, back before sunset" → real route + weather + difficulty + safety brief. Use terrain elevation to grade difficulty and steer first-timers and family campers to the gentlest option — low, firm, flat ground near access points. Caveat: raw elevation ≠ dune difficulty — public data (~30 m) misses individual dunes and dunes shift, so derive slope/ruggedness and blend with community difficulty tags, not elevation alone. Deterministic use of known terrain data — not the hazard "prediction" we deliberately avoid.

    Data strategy: ship a free Copernicus GLO-30 (~30 m) baseline for slope grading now and let community drives refine it — never promise centimetre-accurate dunes. How we source, build and 3D-render that terrain is a play in its own right — see §8.
  3. Arabic-first + local. Full RTL Arabic UI, local place names, SAR/AED pricing, local club partnerships. The incumbents treat Arabic as an afterthought.
  4. Fix the thing Gaia users hate most: offline downloads. Make offline bulletproof and obvious — Gaia's #1 review complaint. Beating them on their core feature is free credibility.
  5. Desert/dune-specific intelligence Gaia/Wikiloc lack: sand conditions, dune difficulty grading, tire-pressure guidance, sunset/heat windows. Let approved UAE club marshals contribute knowledge — text, voice, photo, video, guided Q&A — as verified, trusted input. Mechanism: curate contributions into a searchable knowledge base the AI retrieves from, with marshal-verified entries weighted highest — not naive auto-fine-tuning, which invites hallucination. Process voice/photo/video async in the cloud. This "approved marshal" trust layer is both quality control and the community moat competitors can't copy.
  6. Modern, fast, un-bloated UX (your 4-theme system is already a brand asset) vs. Gaia's "buggy/bloated" reputation and Wikiloc's straight-line-track bugs.
  7. Community with quality, not Wikiloc's uneven dump — verified/curated local trails + your recorder turning real runs into trusted routes.
08
The data moat — living 3D terrain

Map the desert as it's actually driven.

Every recorded drive is a sensor pass. The recorder already logs position, altitude, speed & heading every second — add the phone's barometer and motion sensors and each run also captures the grade and camber of the ground. Enough runs and you've mapped the terrain in 3D — and captured what no off-the-shelf elevation model can: how it actually drives.

  1. Two signals, one mounted phone. Elevation: GPS altitude is rough (±10–20 m); the barometric altimeter in most modern phones gives sub-metre relative precision → a clean up-and-down profile along the driven line. Attitude: the accelerometer + gyroscope give pitch (climb grade) and roll (off-camber), cleanest at steady speed with the two sensors fused and filtered to reject bumps and braking.
  2. Calibrate once per mount, on level ground. Phone fixed vertically in a holder → one "park on flat ground, tap to zero" step learns the mount angle; from then on phone reading − zero = vehicle attitude, no re-calibration unless it's re-seated. On your questions: the phone's own sensors are the spirit level — when still they read tilt to a fraction of a degree, better than a photo ever could, so don't gate calibration on a photo (keep it optional, as a marshal "here's my mount" trust shot). And no — without that zero the phone knows its own tilt vs gravity but not the mount offset, so it can't just assume it's level; the level-ground tap supplies the reference. It can, though, auto-detect a stationary, flat-looking moment and prompt "looks flat — calibrate now?"
  3. Seed with marshals & advanced drivers first. They'll actually follow the mount-and-calibrate protocol, so their runs become high-quality seed data — weighted highest via the same "approved marshal" trust layer. Widen to advanced drivers, then everyone; the crowd averages out noise over popular lines. One track is noisy — 500 passes over the same dune are accurate.
  4. The payoff: a rotatable 3D relief of every trail. The driven line draped over a terrain mesh, coloured by grade and camber — not a literal hologram, but a 3D model you can spin (it fits the 3D map you're already building). And off-camber angle doubles as a live safety signal: "off-camber 22° ahead" is a rollover warning no trail app has.
  5. Buy a stable baseline; let the crowd keep the dunes fresh. You're not drunk — hyper-detailed data exists. Free (~30 m): Copernicus GLO-30, SRTM, ALOS AW3D30 — fine for general relief now. Commercial high-res: Airbus WorldDEM Neo (~5 m) + Pléiades Neo imagery (~30 cm); ALOS AW3D (5 m → 0.5 m); Maxar WorldView (~30 cm); Intermap — sold per km², licence-restricted. UAE-specific: Bayanat / Space42 and MBRSC (KhalifaSat) hold national high-res Earth-observation data — a purchase or partnership could seed the whole country and be a credibility/BD win. The catch that makes crowd-refresh the moat: dunes migrate metres a year, so a survey-grade dune map goes stale — buy high-res for the stable terrain (Hajar Mountains, wadis, rock trails) + a few flagship dune "playgrounds," and let phones continuously refresh the shifting sand.
Don't pay to survey what the desert rewrites every season — buy the mountains, crowdsource the sand.

Pricing recommendation

Don't underprice the Gulf.

The GCC tolerates premium pricing and off-roading is a group activity — so a family/convoy plan lifts ARPU above what solo-hiker apps ever reach. Sell on safety, not map count.

Directional tier structure (localize SAR/AED)
TierPriceGates
Free$0Discovery, browse, community uploads — seeds the trail DB (cold-start fix)
Pro~$39–49/yrOffline, SOS, AI planning, desert intelligence
Family / ConvoypremiumMulti-vehicle live tracking, shared safety — higher ARPU
Why a safety tierSafety converts willingness-to-pay better than maps do. A "Safety" tier justifies a premium people will actually pay for — the map count never will.

Generous free tier for discovery + community uploads, gate offline/SOS/AI behind paid. Worldwide later: match AllTrails/Gaia (~$35.99–39.99/yr) but sell on AI + safety, not layers.
